# Narration and long text (including short-video voiceover)

When to use this one: the words to dub are told to a listener, not said to someone inside the story: narration, a chapter of an audiobook, a long text read aloud, a letter, an announcement or press release, an explanation, meditation guidance, a voice menu, short-video voiceover, from one paragraph to a whole chapter. For one line in a conversation read `scenes/one-line.md`; for a scene where several people take turns read `scenes/a-scene.md`. In a chapter where narration is mixed with dialogue, the narration follows this document and the cues for the dialogue lines follow those two.

## What fit means in this scene

- **Follow the content, or speak to the listener.** A tense passage is tense, a sad one is sad, an ordinary one is ordinary; expressiveness follows the content and is not flattened. It is still told to the listener, not acted as if trading lines with people in the story.
- **Breath between paragraphs.** At a paragraph change or a scene change you can hear a pause.
- **Consistent across the whole chapter.** It is the same person speaking from start to finish; the delivery does not switch midway to a broadcaster tone or to acting against another character; the emotion follows the content and is not one tone for the whole chapter; you cannot hear where it was cut.
- **Told to one person.** Tell it the way this character speaks, not in a broadcaster tone; use broadcaster delivery only when the character is a broadcaster or host.
- **Voiceover speaks to the person watching the video.** More energy than narration and a faster pace, a hook at the start, a firm close at the end; it is still the character's own voice.

## Step by step

There are four means of achieving fit, all below: which reference segment to pick (step 4), what words and cues to write (steps 2 and 3), how much goes into one request (the "Splitting" section), and how to stitch after synthesis (steps 5 and 6).

1. **Decide who reads.**
   - The character narrates (a first-person recollection, a letter the character wrote, the character's own voiceover): pass only this character's pack. Separate the narrated part from the words the character speaks aloud with cues (step 3). When the recollection quotes other people (he said ..., she answered ...), that is also one person playing several roles: borrow only the way `scenes/a-scene.md`「One person playing several roles」 writes role cues (a role cue is a short cue describing a role's voice). The quotation stays inside this paragraph: write the role cue of the person who says it in front of the quote, and after the quote write one for the narrator again to return to the narrator; do not start a new line (step 2, "One paragraph per line").
   - One narrator reads the whole book and also reads the characters' words: still one pack; separate the characters' words with cues, written as in the previous item.
   - A different narrator voice, and each character has their own voice: pass `--pack` several times, with the narrator's pack first; it is the voice for the start of the script and the start of every chapter. Write `[voice:name]` in front of a character's words and `[voice:]` after them to return to the narrator (`references/markup.md`). When the runtime environment declares one request per scene, a passage where several voices take turns is by default synthesized as one scene; for the rules, the trade-offs, and `--no-scene` see `scenes/a-scene.md`.

   Done when: you know how many packs this run takes and which one is first.

2. **Revise the script.** If the source text was written by an author (audiobook, letter, press release, announcement), change words only so that it is read correctly and understood by ear; keep the facts and the wording of the source. Dub the language the source is in: if it is not the language of the pack's references, do not translate it yourself and do not switch packs; `dub` detects it and stops (step 6). If you are asked to write the text (voiceover, promotion, voice menu), write it the way the matching type below describes. What every type needs:
   - One paragraph per line. A non-empty line in the script is a paragraph; when it does not fit and has to be split, the program splits at scene and paragraph boundaries (the "Splitting" section), so a paragraph must be a complete thought: finish an argument, a quotation, or a cause-and-effect before the line break, and do not break a line in the middle of a number, a name, or a quotation.
   - Where the scene changes (a change of time, place, or viewpoint), write a line of its own between the two paragraphs that holds only a pause, for example `[pause 1.5s]` (a stage direction that reads as a pause also counts, for example `（停顿 2 秒）` or `（沉默）`; these are Chinese stage directions, kept as examples). A line that holds only a pause is the boundary of a scene; end the previous paragraph with a period.
   - Write proper names, numbers, abbreviations, and polyphonic characters as the words to be read, the same way from start to end of the chapter. For words the pack's pronunciation lexicon covers, follow the lexicon (`references/dub-details.md`「Pronunciation lexicon」); for words the lexicon does not cover and where you must fix the pronunciation, use a phoneme tag (`models/scene3.md`「Phoneme tags」).
   - Ellipsis: a `……` in the source (common in audiobooks) means hesitation, trailing off; keep it in that sense, between words; do not write it inside a word (`秘……密`: the model judged that 2 times in 9 it broke the word apart; nobody has checked this), do not use `……` alone as a pause, and do not write `。……`, `。[pause]`, or `[long pause]` (with these the reading loses the sentence after them; `models/scene3.md`「Pause between lines」「Punctuation」). Where the source has these, remove the `……` after the period, and where a pause is needed write it as step 5 says.
   - A chapter title (`# title`) is a cut point and is not read; if the chapter name must be read, write it as a separate line under the title.
   - Stage directions (`（叹气）`, `（停顿 2 秒）`) are turned into cues or pauses before sending; the words inside the brackets are not read (`references/markup.md`). When `dub` converts one, it moves the words inside the brackets into square brackets as they are, without shortening: so turn a long stage direction into a cue of two or three words yourself first. `dub` treats every bracket of 30 characters or fewer as a stage direction, and does not read it even when the lexicon does not recognize it: body text inside brackets in the source that is to be read (`会议在 2019 年（疫情之前）举行。`, "The meeting was held in 2019 (before the pandemic).") must first be rewritten without brackets (commas, dashes: `会议在 2019 年，疫情之前，举行。`). Ones you missed: the `notes` of the `--dry-run` plan has an `unrecognized_directions` entry that lists the brackets it did not recognize; but it does not list all of them: a bracket that contains a word from the lexicon (`（笑话比赛）` contains 「笑」, "laugh") is not in it, and is also not read, so check every place yourself.

   Then by purpose:
   - **Audiobooks and long text** Split into paragraphs as the source does; do not merge, do not break up.
   - **News and announcements** Factual accuracy comes first; state time, place, and what the listener has to do clearly. Write dates, times, road names, flight numbers, alert levels, and phone numbers the way they are read ("13:05" written as "十三点零五分", the time spelled out in Chinese words; a phone number digit by digit).
   - **Explanation and teaching** Turn phrases that depend on the eyes ("as shown", "below", "the one on the left"; in Chinese 「如图」「如下」「左边这个」) into descriptions that make sense by ear; one step per paragraph, explained step by step; at the first appearance of a term, write how it is read and then use it.
   - **Accessible reading aloud** Express headings, lists, quotations, and paragraph levels with spoken connectors ("item one", "the quotation follows", "end of quotation"; in Chinese 「第一条」「下面是引文」「引文完」); for a web address read only the useful domain, or say clearly where the link goes; for a table first say what it is about and which items it has, then summarize; write numbers, dates, currency, percentages, and units as the words to be read, following custom.
   - **Meditation guidance** Mostly short sentences, a gentle pace; write the blank space as `[pause …]` and state how long; soft wording and soft cues.
   - **Voice menus** Minimal; say what to do first and then the information ("To check your balance, press 1"; in Chinese 「查询余额，请按 1」); one option per sentence, in a clear order. When each prompt must be a separate file, run one `dub` per prompt.
   - **Short-video voiceover** The first sentence grabs the listener; colloquial, as if talking to a person in front of the camera; state turns clearly ("but", "as a result"; in Chinese 「可是」「结果」); the last sentence closes it, with a summary or a call to action.
   - **Promotion and branding** Short sentences, a brisk pace; state the call to action clearly, what the listener should do; turn written style into spoken style.

   Done when: every paragraph is one line and one complete thought; every scene change has a line that holds only a pause; proper names and numbers are the words to be read; every stage direction has been turned into a cue or a pause; every ellipsis is between words and none is used alone as a pause.

3. **Write cues: write one when the wording changes, each short and exact.** First follow `models/scene3.md`「Where a cue is written and how far it reaches」›「Set the state first, then write the cue.」 Use every form the vendor documentation lists, in the right place (`models/scene3.md`「Cues」). Write them as `models/scene3.md`「Good examples」shows: several short cues in one line, written where the delivery changes. At the very start of each line write the character's name first (`[甘雨]`, written in the language of this line's words), ahead of all cues (`models/scene3.md`「Put the character name first」; in the pack's own language it is the pack's `name`, written the same).
   - Write one at the start of each paragraph, saying how this paragraph is told; if it is the same as the previous paragraph, write it again anyway: whether a cue still applies across paragraphs has not been tested (`models/scene3.md`「Where a cue is written and how far it reaches」). When a paragraph is so long that it is cut inside the paragraph (`--dry-run` shows where), write one in front of the sentence after the cut as well.
   - Places inside a paragraph where the state changes: where the telling reaches something important, where it relaxes after that, and a sentence the character speaks aloud inside the narration (for the state at this place, write it whether or not the pack has acting style (`### 演法`, acting style); how to write it is in `scenes/one-line.md` step 3).
   - For a few words only, wrap those words in paired tags (`models/scene3.md`「Paired tags」): a name or a number the listener must remember, `<slow>…</slow>` or `<emphasis>…</emphasis>`; a whispered quoted sentence, `<whisper>…</whisper>`; a half-sentence turned soft in meditation or a bedtime story, `<soft>…</soft>`; a shout to stop in the story, `<forceful>…</forceful>`: the one shouted at is often another person in the story, so the role cue in front and the one written after the shout to return to the narrator both follow step 1; the tag wraps only the words that are forced: `[low, gravelly, stern]<forceful>站住！</forceful>[calm, slowing down]那人愣在了原地。` (`low, gravelly` is the role cue of the person shouting; an example of the notation, not tested). For a few words quoted in another language inside the narration, wrap them in a language tag (`<french>…</french>`, same section, "Language tags"). A momentary sound (a sigh, a laugh, a breath in) is written at the place where it happens (same section, "Sudden sounds and pauses").
   - Each cue is two or three words that say clearly the emotion or the delivery at this place; always write them in English, whatever the language of the line; do not write them as sentences. What this paragraph is about and why it is told this way do not go into the square brackets; write how it lands on the voice. Who it is told to does not go into the square brackets either (`models/scene3.md`「What to write」). For example:

     ```
     [calm, slowing down]那年冬天，雪下得特别大。[calm, hushed voice]半夜里，院门忽然响了。
     [tense, hushed voice]父亲披上衣服，[tense, very quiet]一步一步挪到门口。[tense, breath held]<whisper>谁？</whisper>
     [relieved, exhaling]门外站着的，是走丢了三天的小黄狗。
     ```

     News `[clear, steady]`, meditation `[gentle, soothing]`, voiceover `[lively, smiling voice]`. This passage and these few are examples of the notation and have not been tested.
   - Set the strength by the content at this place: the stronger the cue, the more intense the acting (inference: on the first-page audition one person listened blind; one group heard the difference in strength, and another group with a different seed heard the two versions as about the same; `models/scene3.md`「Readings from the first-page audition」). Write a tense passage as tense and an ordinary one as ordinary; do not write everything mild to be safe. For a sentence that has been restrained throughout, where sadness rises and is then held down again, write it as the cue that was deleted in the second example of `models/scene3.md`「Good examples」 (inference, only for this one sentence).
   - When the selected setup sets an emotion, style, or energy and a line starts without a cue, `dub` combines them into one cue and adds it at the start of that request (the "Splitting" section; `--setup` in `references/dub.md`). If every paragraph starts with a cue (a character name counts too), the setup's cue cannot be added: when the setup fixes the base tone, write the base tone after the name. The setup's notes (for example the features of an accent) are for you; `dub` does not send them to the model: write them concretely in the script as they appear, at the place where they happen. Write accents as `models/scene3.md`「Cross-language and accent」 says: when crossing languages write the name pair at the start; write a dialect as its own words, and do not write the name of the dialect (things like `[带一点四川口音]`, "with a slight Sichuan accent": a Chinese cue that names the dialect, which you must not write). The setup's speaking speed still applies.
   - Expressiveness: when the voice has a fixed value (from the setup, or the voice's global value, visible in the plan as `pack.expressiveness`, and for several packs as `packs[i].expressiveness`), by default do not pass `--expressiveness`; pass it only with a reason, and move about 10 from the fixed value. When it is not fixed (neither place has one), by default pass `--expressiveness`, choosing the value by `models/scene3.md`「Expressiveness」›「How to choose the value」. When one chapter needs different values, use the per-item fields of a `.json` script. When the narrator and the characters use their own packs and some are fixed and some are not, do not use `--expressiveness` (it overrides the fixed ones) and write it, following `models/scene3.md`「Expressiveness」›「How to choose the value」, only on the items of the voices that are not fixed. For several voices with different fixed values, lines with different values are not merged into one request; to merge them, give one value explicitly and do not average. Write the number in that one place only.
   - For an overall speed that must be exact, change speed after synthesis: for the whole thing a bit slower (meditation, a bedtime story), pass `--speed <factor>` (for example `--speed 0.9`, which overrides the setup's speaking speed, `references/dub.md`「Opening cue and speed」), or use a setup that has such a speed. For a voiceover that must match the length of the video, use the target duration (step 6). When the telling itself speeds up or slows down in this paragraph, write it in the same acting note as the emotion of this paragraph (`[calm, slowing down]`, `[tense, hurried]`, `models/scene3.md`「What to write」); when only a few words should slow down, use `<slow>` as above. Do not write a separate speed cue to adjust speed, and do not change punctuation or add pauses to adjust rhythm: both were listened to and neither sounded good (`models/scene3.md`「Speed and pauses: conclusions from listening」).
   - The few extras added when dubbing (a short stop after a comma is written `，[pause 0.6s]`, a sigh is written `[sighs]`): the notation, where to pause, and the basis are in `models/scene3.md`「Add a few more things when dubbing.」. Those extras were listened to only on lines spoken by one person and not on narration or long text; for pauses in narration see also step 5.

   Done when: every clause has had its state set and every place where the state changes has a cue; every paragraph start has a cue; a few words that change alone use paired tags; no cue is longer than 4 words (target two or three), none is written as a sentence, and none was written mild to be safe; a voice with a fixed expressiveness has none passed, unless there is a reason and it moved about 10 from the fixed value.

4. **Decide the reference.** In this order, use the first one that exists:
   1. The user wants a different reference segment (you were asked to change it, or the user named a reference tag): `--reference-tag <tag>`.
   2. The selected setup names a default reference: pass nothing; `dub` uses it by itself.
   3. Neither: look at what this chapter is about, pick the closest-fitting reference tag from those the pack's `SKILL.md` lists, and give it to `dub` with `--reference-tag`. If the character is a teenager or old in this chapter, pick that one; for a voiceover pick a brisk one. The acting style follows the reference (so heard blind by one person on two groups on the first page; for other voices it is inference, `models/scene3.md`「Reference segments」), so pick by the content of this chapter. If nothing fits, pass none, and `dub` uses the default segment of the language of the line.

   In one `dub` run a pack uses one tag only, and the blocks it is cut into use the same reference segment; the exception is blocks where the language changes between lines, which use a segment of that language (the "Always split" item of the "Splitting" section). To change the tag midway, see the "Common pitfalls" section. The default reference a setup names is only a default: change it through `--reference-tag` when the user wants another, or when you hear the timbre has drifted.

   Done when: in the `--dry-run` report the `pick` of every block is the segment you want, and the reason matches.

5. **Write the pauses.**
   - Within one request, the breath between paragraphs is decided by the model, and reaches at most the limit the model leaves (`models/scene3.md`「Pause between lines」).
   - For a longer breath (a scene change, the end of a section, blank space in meditation), write a pause such as `[pause 1.5s]` there; at a scene change it is the line of its own written in step 2. If it is longer than what fits in one request (the limit the runtime environment declares, or the base part's default when none is declared), `dub` cuts there (`references/dub.md`「One request per passage」), and at stitching inserts silence of the same length: as long as you wrote, so long it stops. Where the text must be read straight on, write the pause within the length that fits. A cut that falls in silence is harder to hear than one that falls in the middle of a passage (inference, not tested).
   - A short pause inside one request follows `models/scene3.md`「Pause between lines」; do not use `……` alone as a pause, and do not write `。……`, `。[pause]`, or `[long pause]` (with these the reading loses the sentence after them, same section).
   - A pause written as a square-bracket cue (`[沉默片刻]`, `[停了一下]`; Chinese cues meaning "a moment of silence" and "paused for a moment") is not treated by `dub` as a pause: it inserts no silence there and does not cut there, and hands it to the model as it is; whether it pauses and for how long is up to the model. What was measured is only `[停顿]` written inside one sentence, and it is unstable; the two ways of writing it at a speaker change in a merged scene are only model-judged, and in inference also unstable (`models/scene3.md`「Pause between lines」). To get a pause, write `[pause …]`, or write a stage direction in round brackets that reads as a pause (`（沉默）`, step 2), and treat it as in the two items above.

   Done when: every pause longer than one breath is written as `[pause …]` with its length.

6. **Dub the narration.**
   1. `--dry-run` first: see how many blocks it was cut into, where, and which reference segment each uses; it costs nothing. If the cut points are not what you expected, go back to step 2 and change the paragraphing. If it reports `dub_cross_language` (the reference segment used is not in the language of the passage, for example a Japanese voice reading an English chapter) nothing has been dubbed: do not ask the user and do not add a reference; write the lines in the target language, write the name pair `[name in the reference language|name in the line's language]` at the start of each line, add `--allow-cross-language`, and dub again, and the whole chapter is handled this way (`references/character-voice.md`「Language and accent」). If the report says the pack actually has a segment in this language (`hasLanguage` is `true`), no reference needs to be added; change it the way the report says, and the way depends on who supplied the value. If you supplied it (the `--reference-tag` you picked in step 4, or the `--language` given in the "Splitting" section to keep it in one request), switch to a tag that exists in this language or drop `--language`, and tell the user together. If the user named the tag (item 1 of step 4), ask him whether to switch to a tag that exists in this language or to leave the tag unspecified (`dub` uses the tag the caller gave first; adding `--language`, switching `--setup`, and `--no-setup` all still stop, do not list them for him). If the setup wrote it, ask him whether to change it: for a language the setup wrote, override it with `--language <language of the lines>`; for the setup's default reference, override it by pointing `--reference-tag` at a tag that exists in this language; for both you may switch to another `--setup`, or use `--no-setup`. When the narration and the characters are merged into one scene, follow step 6 of `scenes/a-scene.md`.
   2. Look at the first line of the text output of `--dry-run`: the summary, which says how many characters will be read, how many requests, and which model. When it is split into several requests, first tell the user "about so many characters, dubbed in so many runs" (when the summary says "this time p will be synthesized", say p runs); when the adapter that is connected stated a cost (`billing`), add "every run is billed"; the user says go and you dub. When dubbing chapter by chapter, add up the summaries of the chapters and say it once at the start. Do not say how long it will take: the duration has not been measured.
   3. One script per chapter, one `dub`, then `mix` into one file (`references/dub.md`「Stitching」). If the chapters must be delivered one file per chapter (common for audiobooks), dub chapter by chapter.
   4. A voiceover that must match the length of the video: pass `--target-duration-ms` (`references/dub.md`「Target duration」). The target duration applies to one piece of audio, so this voiceover must fit in one request; if it is cut, `dub_input_invalid` is reported before any money is spent. Do not write chapter titles, voice changes, or pauses that do not fit in a request in it, and do not change language between lines (the "Always split" item of the "Splitting" section). The cues written at the start of each paragraph in step 3 differ from paragraph to paragraph and do not cause a cut; when a setup with a cue is selected and a cue was written earlier but some later line starts without one, it is cut there (the "A setup with a cue is selected" item of the "Splitting" section), so write a cue at the start of every line.

   Done when: if it was split into several requests, the user said go; `dub` exits 0 and `mix` produced the file.

7. **Read the report, then hand it back.** Do not check separately after dubbing: do not transcribe each block and compare with the script, and do not switch tags and redo by similarity. The failure and bad-output signals that `dub` reports itself (`failed`, `speech_maybe_unfinished`, `target_duration_missed`, the platform cutting it off) are not covered by this: handle them by the "Common pitfalls" section and `references/dub.md`「Failure and redo」「Whether it was read in full」, because those are things that cannot be handed over; `speech_maybe_unfinished` is the exception: it only reports which block may have been cut, does not redo automatically, and you tell the user as it is and the user decides. In the note handed back, write which places this run are worth the user's listening:
   - The start and end of each block: a block is one generation, and the model gives no error when a sentence in the middle is missing (a sentence after an ellipsis or a long pause was lost: a lone `……`, `。……`, `。[pause]`, `[long pause]` are readings, see step 2; an ellipsis between words is model-judged, see below; other cases have not been tested), when it stopped before finishing (the audio length was measured, but whether it finished is from the model's transcription, which nobody has checked, `models/scene3.md`「Limits of one request」), or when the first character at the start was not spoken (not tested). The blocks whose `notes` in `dub.json` have `speech_maybe_unfinished` came out shorter than half of the estimate from the character count; redo them as the "Skipped sentences, early stop" item of the "Common pitfalls" section says (`references/dub.md`「Whether it was read in full」); the absence of this entry does not mean everything was read in full, so ask the user to listen to the start and end of each block.
   - Timbre: when the runtime environment has the speaker similarity capability (`speaker-verification`), `units[i].speakerSimilarity` of each block in `dub.json` records which reference segment it was compared with (`segmentId`) and how similar (`similarity`). If `--min-similarity <number>` was given when dubbing, a block below it has `drifted` set to `true`; the default threshold is not set yet, and without it there is only the number, whose range depends on the adapter's notes. When the top-level `speakerSimilarity.measured` is `false`, it was not measured this run. Segments merged into one scene cannot be measured; to measure, use `--no-scene` (`references/dub.md`「Comparing timbre」). The numbers and `drifted` stay in `dub.json` as they are, and are not a reason to redo it yourself; when the user asks whether the timbre stays stable over dozens of paragraphs of a long text, follow the `similarity` of each block and say it as it is, and when he hears a drift, handle it as the "The voice drifted" item of the "Common pitfalls" section. For a merged scene, after dubbing tell the user as step 8 of `scenes/a-scene.md` says: "These were dubbed together; if a timbre does not sound like the character, the lines can be redone one by one separately." and redo those scenes with `--no-scene` if the user wants.
   - Sentences that use an ellipsis: say so when handing back, that the words after an ellipsis are occasionally lost (model-judged, nobody has checked, `models/scene3.md`「Punctuation」); one in the middle of a block is the same, inference, and several in a row have not been tested; ask the user to listen to these sentences. Audiobook source often carries `……`, and every one kept counts.
   - Seams: where it was cut, tell the user which places it was cut and ask him to listen before and after the cut points.

   Done when: the note handed back says to ask the user to listen to the start and end of each block; for sentences that use an ellipsis it says the words after may occasionally be lost (model-judged, nobody has checked); when it was cut, it names the cut points; for merged scenes it tells the user they can be redone separately; when `dub` reported a failure or bad-output signal, it was handled by the "Common pitfalls" section; you did not transcribe each block yourself and did not redo by similarity; when the top-level `speakerSimilarity.measured` is `false` (not measured), you do not need to say so each time, and say that it was not measured only when the user asks whether the timbre is stable or hears a drift.

## Splitting: do not split if it fits, otherwise split only at boundaries

In this version `dub` splits like this:

- If a chapter fits in one request (next item), it is spoken in one request. Apart from the "Always split" cases below, a text that is not long is not split.
- By default one request is about 1000 characters (of what is read: cues and tag names are not counted, consecutive whitespace counts as one, about 170 words in English; for how they are counted see `references/dub.md`「How long text is split」), cut at paragraphs and speaker changes. Do not add `--max-chars` yourself: it counts the characters that are sent (cues and tags included), which is not the way the default counts, so for a script with many cues and tags `--max-chars 1000` cuts finer than the default. A scene where the narration and the characters are synthesized together is the same: when long, `dub` cuts it between lines into several scenes. When the adapter declares a maximum number of characters per request, it does not go above that either. For why 1000, see `models/scene3.md`「Limits of one request」; it also says how much the model itself can hold in one request: the longer and the more numerous the references, the shorter what can be said. Limits the platform adds (how long the gateway waits) depend on the adapter's notes.
- When it does not fit, first cut at the boundary of a scene: a line in the chapter that holds only a pause (step 2). When it fits, this line stays in the request as it is, and the pause is also in the request (when the pause itself is longer than what fits in a request, follow "Always split" below). If there is no such line, or it still does not fit after cutting there, cut at paragraph boundaries, which means between lines. If one line does not fit by itself, cut at sentence boundaries within this line, and say so in the report. For the details of the program see the place in `references/dub.md` that covers splitting.
- When `dub` cuts by itself (it does not fit, or in the cases below under "Always split" and "A setup with a cue is selected", except chapters and voice changes) and the script has no pause written at the cut, `dub` records a default pause at the cut (about half a second, which `mix` inserts as silence; for the number and its source see `references/dub.md`「How long text is split」›「Breath pause at the cut.」), so a cut also has a breath. For another length, write `[pause …]` at the cut. With `--no-scene`, one line per request, nothing is added between lines and the script's pauses apply.
- The blocks cut because it did not fit use the same reference segment and the same seed: when the runtime environment accepts a seed, `dub` gives it a fixed value and writes it in the report. Whether the same seed makes the blocks sound closer to each other has not been tested (`models/scene3.md`「Sampling: seed」).
- Always split: a chapter title (`# title`; a line shorter than `limits.minChars` is the exception, see the end of the next item), a voice change, a long pause that does not fit in one request (step 5), and a language change between lines (the pack has a default segment of that language and the reference segment used is different; in the report the `cutBefore` of that block is `language` and `notes` has a `language_cut` entry). A short passage of mixed-language speech (an English sentence quoted in Chinese narration) must stay in one request: write it in the same line (a line picks only one reference segment, the words in the other language are wrapped in a language tag, `models/scene3.md`「Language tags」; the other language inside the line follows this line and does not stop); or pass `--language` (the whole script picks references by this one language), or pass a `--reference-tag` that exists in only one language: with these two, the whole line in the other language uses this reference segment, which is cross-language cloning and often carries an accent (some of the voices listened to had an audible accent, the others are inference), and you must add `--allow-cross-language`, otherwise `dub` stops. When written in the same line, the whole line is still Chinese, and the English sentence inserted follows the Chinese reference, and `dub` does not stop; `dub` stops only when a whole line changes language and the reference segment used is not in this language (the pack has no such language, or `--language` or `--reference-tag` was given) (step 6).
- A setup with a cue is selected (step 3) also causes a cut: if the previous request has a cue written by the script (at the start of a line or inside a sentence) and the next line has none, the next line starts another request and the setup's cue is added back at its start (`references/dub.md`「Opening cue and speed」). If the previous request is the setup's cue from start to end, it is merged as before. If every paragraph starts with a cue (step 3), this item does not apply: a line that has its own cue written does not get the setup's added, and is merged as before. When the environment declares a minimum number of characters per request (`limits.minChars`), a line that is too short is still merged into the next line, across chapters too: the cue at the start of the merged request is computed from the short line (if it wrote its own cue at the start it is not added), and the next line does not get the setup's cue added (`references/dub.md`「One request per passage」›「Ones shorter than the minimum are still merged.」).
- A block cannot hear the previous block: the blocks are joined only by the same reference segment, the same seed, and the cue at the start of each block. So the fewer cut points the better, and cut them at the boundaries of scenes and paragraphs.

## Common pitfalls

- **A long request is cut off by the platform** (gateway timeout, for example HTTP 524): the model can hold it, but that does not mean the platform can wait for it. Add a chapter title (`# title`, not read) at a scene or paragraph boundary in the middle of the failed block to cut it into two blocks (if what follows the title is not spoken by the first pack, rewrite `[voice:name]` at the start of the line under the title; for a breath, write `[pause 0.5s]`; see `references/dub.md`「How long text is split」›「A piece cannot hear the previous one.」), then run the same `dub` command again: the unchanged blocks are reused as they are and no money is spent twice (`references/dub.md`「Failure and redo」). If your command did not wait long enough and `dub` was interrupted, run the same command again too: the blocks that were finished before the interruption are reused as well. When the model says a request does not fit, cut it the same way.
- **Skipped sentences, early stop** (`dub` reported `speech_maybe_unfinished`: it only reports which block may have been cut, does not redo automatically, you tell the user and the user decides; or the user wants it redone, or has heard it himself): redo the same block (`--only <index>`, output directory as before; if you run the same command without `--only`, this block is reused as it is and not synthesized again). When the runtime environment accepts a seed, the block that `--only` points to gets a different seed (the previous one plus 1, `references/dub.md`「Failure and redo」): the same seed and the same input are declared by the platform to give the same result as far as possible, and the program compared one pair and it was the same; a different seed may differ, also compared on this one pair only. If it still skips or stops with a different seed, change the input and dub again: cut that block smaller as in the previous item, or change the punctuation and paragraphing of those sentences.
- **The voice drifted** (the user heard it and wants it changed; `drifted` being `true` and the `similarity` of the blocks getting lower toward the end are numbers for the user to see, not a reason to redo it yourself): follow what the user says, switch to another reference tag and dub again (`--reference-tag`); when the setup names a default reference it can be changed too. In a long text, change it for the whole chapter together: `--only` can redo only the blocks that drifted, but those blocks use a different reference from their neighbors, and the seams are easier to hear (inference). Which one to switch to: pick again as in step 4, choosing another one that is closest to the character's usual voice.
- **A reference tag must change midway** (for example the narration reaches the character's childhood and must switch to the "teenager" segment): in this version the script has no marker to change the tag by line, in one `dub` run a pack uses only one tag, and `dub` does not accept the same pack twice. At the place to change (choose the boundary of a paragraph or a scene) split the script into two files, run `dub` once for each with its own `--reference-tag`, then `mix <first output directory> <second output directory> --out <file.wav>`, which joins them into one file in the order given. `mix` adds no pause at the split (`dub` adds one when it cuts at a paragraph boundary itself, but not here); for a breath, write `[pause 0.5s]` at the start of the second script; the line that holds only a pause at a scene boundary can stay in either file. The same split applies when the character narrates and the words he speaks aloud need another tag.
- **Read in a broadcaster tone**: change the cue at the start of each paragraph to the way of speaking (`[relaxed, smiling voice]`, an example of the notation, not tested; do not write the name of a way of telling such as "casual" or "chatty", the first item of `models/scene3.md`「What to write」), and pick for the reference the segment where the character speaks in the usual way: if the reference is read flatly, the performance also comes out flat (heard by one person on the first page, for one character, the others are inference, `models/scene3.md`「Reference segments」); a reading tone in the reference, in inference, carries into every sentence after (only the model judged whether it sounds like reading aloud, nobody has listened to it, `references/character-voice.md`「Default segment: a passage the character says naturally, with acting」).
- **One tone for the whole chapter, tense parts passed flatly**: the cue is written only at the start of the chapter, or each paragraph has the same one. Follow step 3: at the start of each paragraph write it by the content, and write another where the delivery changes inside the paragraph; if the reference is read flatly the performance may also come out flat (heard by one person on the first page, for one character, the others are inference); follow step 4 and pick another segment by the content.
- **A scene of narration and characters synthesized together is too long or changes voice**: beyond the default of about 1000 characters, `dub` cuts it itself between lines into several scenes (the "Splitting" section); if the platform still says it does not fit after cutting, it is not cut again automatically and is recorded as a failure. Merging may also swap someone's timbre. As `scenes/a-scene.md` says, switch to `--no-scene`, or add a chapter title to cut it. `--no-scene` is one request per line (exception: when the adapter declares a minimum number of characters per request, a line shorter than it is still merged into the next line, see `--no-scene` in `references/dub.md`); the several lines the narrator continues with are also dubbed separately and there are many cut points, so use it only where the speaker changes often in those scenes (split into a separate script), and for the whole chapter still add chapter titles to cut.

## Which model notes are used

All in `models/scene3.md`:

- "Cues", under it "Where a cue is written and how far it reaches": a cue takes effect from where it is written; set the state of each clause first and then write the cue; whether it reaches across paragraphs has not been tested. Step 3.
- "What to write": write concrete words, not summary labels such as accent or manner of telling (first item); one per place, or two short ones side by side, two or three words, not written as a sentence, always written in English, and the acting note may carry speed. Step 3, and the broadcaster tone in the pitfalls.
- "Cues", under it "Add a few more things when dubbing.": how to write a short stop or a sigh inside one person's lines, and the readings; not listened to on narration. Step 3.
- "Cross-language and accent": a dialect is written as the words of that dialect, and the name of the dialect is not written. Step 3.
- "Sudden sounds and pauses", "Paired tags", "Language tags", "Phoneme tags": how to write the delivery of a few words, a quoted other language, and a specified pronunciation; the effect of the slow-down notation could not be measured (readings, write it anyway). Steps 2 and 3.
- "Good examples": several short cues in one line, written where the delivery changes. Step 3.
- "Text in round brackets is read aloud": the model reads the words inside round brackets (a platform declaration), while `dub` treats every bracket of 30 characters or fewer as a stage direction and does not read it: turn a stage direction into a cue, and rewrite body text in brackets in the source that is to be read without brackets. Step 2.
- "Readings from the first-page audition": cue strength (on the first page one person listened blind, one group heard the strength, one group sounded about the same); set the strength by the content at this place. Step 3.
- "Reference segments": the acting style follows the reference, and a flatly read reference also comes out flat (one person listened on the first page, the other voices are inference). Step 4, and the broadcaster tone and one tone in the pitfalls.
- "Pause between lines": how long a pause in one request is, and why a long pause is inserted at stitching. Step 5.
- "Expressiveness": a control parameter, the higher the value the more drama; the order of the three layers (global, setup, at `dub` time); a voice with a fixed value gets none passed by default, one that is not fixed can get a value, and the starting point and how to adjust up and down are there. Step 3.
- "Sampling: seed": `seed` was tested on one pair only. The "Splitting" section, and skipped sentences in the pitfalls.
- "Limits of one request": how the default of about 1000 characters per request was set; whether it fits depends on the length of the references and the text, and what comes back when it does not fit. The "Splitting" section, and a long request in the pitfalls.
- "Speaker markers": used when narration and characters are synthesized as one scene (`scenes/a-scene.md`).
