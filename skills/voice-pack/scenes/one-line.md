# One line in a dialogue

**When to use this document.** A character speaks one line or a few lines to someone, in the middle of a conversation: one reply in role-play, one line in a work, one message dubbed into one audio. When the dubbing does not say which scene it is, dub following this document and treat it as role-play with emotion. A line dubbed for a video, with the duration matched to the picture, also follows this document, with step 5 added. If this line is part of a scene (several characters take turns and the result is one whole passage), read `scenes/a-scene.md`; if it is part of narration or a long text, read `scenes/narration.md`; for the lines in those two documents where a character speaks for themselves, write the cues following step 3 of this document. For other scenes see the base part's `SKILL.md`「Dubbing by scene」.

This document is written after `models/scene3.md`, and every place that depends on it names the section in brackets. When the connected adapter maps dubbing to another model, first read that model's model notes (`models/`), and where they do not match this document, follow them.

## What fit means

The listener feels that the character is talking to someone, not reciting lines:

- **Emotion and intent at this moment**: why he says this line, and what he wants the other person to do.
- **Distance from the other person**: whispering close up, or shouting across a courtyard.
- **Spoken language**: modal particles, hesitation, changing course halfway through a sentence.
- **No drift**: after dozens of lines it is still the same person's voice.
- **Read only the line**: stage directions, actions and the speaker's name are not read aloud.

There are four things you can move: which reference segment to use (step 4); what words and cues to write (steps 2 and 3); how much goes into one request: the words of this one message are one passage, spoken in one request (it is cut at a long pause that does not fit in a request, step 2), and other lines are not put in ("Common pitfalls"); and how it is stitched after synthesis: long pauses, speed changes to match the picture, joining into one file (steps 5 and 8).

## Steps

1. **Read the pack.** Read the pack's `SKILL.md`: the last section, the dubbing guidance (`### 这个人是谁` (who this person is), `### 音色` (timbre), `### 演法` (acting style), `### 台词` (lines), `### 换语言时怎么说` (what to say when changing language)), and the reference tags listed by language. How he would say this line, his attitude toward the person in front of him, and which way his voice goes when emotion rises are decided from "这个人是谁"; when written in another language, the name, the way he refers to himself and his catchphrases follow "换语言时怎么说". An item in the guidance that is written `缺` (missing) has no basis; say honestly that you decided it. Then decide which setup to use this time: if the user named one, use that one (`--setup <name>`); if not, it is the one marked `default`, and if there is none, use no setup; if the user said not to use a setup, give `--no-setup` (the one marked `default` is not applied either). Setups are in `setups` in the pack's `manifest.json`: they record its language (`labels.language`), emotion, style, energy, notes (`instructions`, for example the features of an accent, step 3 item 6), speech rate and default reference (`referenceTag`). When the caller gives no language, `dub` picks the reference by the setup's language. If it does not match the language of the line and the script makes this visible (the setup says `en`, the line is Chinese), `dub` stops (step 6); for several languages written in the same script (the setup says `fr`, the line is English, both in Latin letters), `dub` cannot tell, picks the reference by the setup's language and does not stop, and `pick.crossLanguage` is also `false`: check it yourself, and if they do not match, dub per step 6, the same as when `dub` stops.

   Done when: you know which setup is used (or none), and have noted these items of it.

2. **Revise the script.** Keep only the words the character says aloud, written the way they can be said face to face:
   1. When a message mixes narration and lines, dub only the lines (the words in quotation marks); the expression and tone described in the narration are kept for step 3 and written as cues. Remove the speaker's name at the start of a line (`林晚：`, a Chinese name and colon), or it will be read aloud (inference: these characters are sent to the model as they are, not tested specifically).
   2. Stage directions: those that say how something is said (`（叹气）`, `*压低声音*`: "(sighs)", "*lowers voice*") are replaced by square-bracket cues, placed before the words they govern (`[sighs]`, `[hushed voice]`); actions unrelated to the manner of speaking (`*走到窗边*`: "*walks to the window*") are deleted. The model reads words in round brackets anyway (`models/scene3.md`「Text in round brackets is read aloud」); `dub`, however, treats all brackets of up to 30 characters as stage directions, and does not read the ones the vocabulary cannot recognize either: for words to be read in the source's brackets, first change them to a form without brackets (commas, dashes). Before sending, `dub` also replaces stage directions with English cues (`（叹气）` becomes `[sighs]`; `（慢慢地）`, which only says fast or slow, is not written as a cue, `references/dub.md`「How a line becomes synthesis」step 2); replacing them yourself lets you write the meaning more exactly.
   3. Make it spoken: write following the "台词" section of the dubbing guidance (how he refers to himself, sentence endings, catchphrases); change written language into words that can be said; use ellipses, dashes and repeated characters as the acting requires, written following `models/scene3.md`「Punctuation」: write hesitation as `……` (in English `...`), placed between words, not in the middle of a word, and do not use `……` alone as a pause, and do not write `。……`, `。[pause]` or `[long pause]` (with these ways of writing, the readings show the following sentence being dropped, `models/scene3.md`「Pause between lines」); for stuttering and restarting halfway, repeat the character and write `，` or `……` in between (`我……我不是那个意思`, "I... I didn't mean that"); for a half-sentence to be said in one breath, remove the comma (what was measured is fewer pauses and a shorter whole sentence; whether it sounds urgent has not been listened to by anyone, inference); for a drawn-out ending write `～` at the end of the sentence (in English `~`). A `～` in the middle of a sentence has not been tested (`models/scene3.md`「Punctuation」, the last item, "Not measured"): for a drawn-out word in mid-sentence such as after a form of address (`师父啊～走了一整天了`), either move the drawn-out words to the end of the whole sentence (`走了一整天了，师父啊～`), or keep the comma as it was and write the drawl as acting direction (`[stretching the words]师父啊，[aggrieved]走了一整天了`). For one clear pause write something like `[pause 0.6s]`, no longer than the `limits.maxPauseInRequestMs` the adapter declares (if none is declared, the base part's default), and the pause is written inside the request (`models/scene3.md`「Pause between lines」). A pause longer than that is cut by `dub` at that point (`references/dub.md`「One request per passage」) and silence is inserted when stitching; so where the reading should run straight on, write the pause within the length that fits. After a cut, the later request does not carry the earlier cues: if it should still be said that way after the pause, write it again after the pause (same section).
   4. Reading: write proper names, numbers, abbreviations and polyphonic characters the way they are to be read, without changing the meaning (a score `3:2` is written `三比二`, "three to two", spelled out in Chinese characters). The pack's pronunciation lexicon is substituted by `dub` (`references/dub-details.md`「Pronunciation lexicon」); for ones not in the lexicon that are easy to misread, change them in the script, or give the reading with phoneme tags (for a polyphonic character `<|phoneme_start|>hang2<|phoneme_end|>`; how to write it: `models/scene3.md`「Phoneme tags」).
   5. Punctuation: set it for the effect you want to hear, not by written grammar (`models/scene3.md`「Punctuation」). A comma is a breath; with a single Chinese question mark the model mostly judges the sentence end to fall, and when the sentence has no question word the question mostly cannot be heard (both are leads, nobody has listened), so write the questioning tone as how the voice concretely changes at this spot, and do not write names of sentence types such as `[反问]` or `[疑问]` ("rhetorical question", "question") (`models/scene3.md`「What to write」); for a sentence with a question word write `？？`, and the sentence end is higher than with a single `？` (measured 1.1 semitones higher, 7/9; both kinds of sentence end are still lower than the median of the whole sentence; only one sentence was tested, nobody has listened; `？？` on a sentence without a question word has not been tested); for English questions choose by the tone wanted: a single `?` sounds like challenging, `?!` sounds like surprise or doubt (listened to by one person, `models/scene3.md`「Punctuation」); with a single `?` the measured sentence end goes up, and the pair with the largest difference, as heard by one person, was audible, while the model judged it rising only 4/9 (lead); `?!` measures a higher rise, and the model also judged it rising (7/9, lead); for a sentence that could also be a question as written (`你明天还来！`, "You are coming again tomorrow!"), add a single `！`, and the model often judged it as sounding like a question (lead); write excitement and shouting into the cue; Chinese `！！！` makes the sentence end lower than a single `！` and occasionally adds an extra sound (1 out of 9 times, model judgment, nobody has checked), so do not write it; for an abrupt stop after being interrupted, none of the punctuation tested measured it (the measured duration and sentence end are about the same; whether an abrupt stop was detected is only the model's judgment, lead; nobody has listened).
   6. Add no facts and delete none, do not change the language, and leave the script markup (`references/markup.md`) as it is. If the lines the user gave are not in the same language as the pack's references (the pack has only Japanese, the lines are English), write them as they are as well, do not translate them into the pack's language yourself, and do not switch to another pack: when the languages do not match, dub per step 3 item 7 (step 6). If the lines are translated (localized dubbing): make them sound natural in the target language, make the pauses follow the habits of the target language, settle the readings of proper names and terms first, and do not change the original meaning.
   7. Merge short lines into the one before or after: when the dubbing model has a minimum number of characters (stated in that model's model notes; or the adapter declares `limits.minChars`), every passage sent for dubbing on its own must reach at least that number. Write short exclamations and replies ("嗯。", "走！": Chinese examples, "Mm.", "Go!") into the same line as the previous or the next one ("嗯。我知道了，你先回去吧。", "Mm. I understand, you go back first."), and write their own cues in their places as usual. `dub` also merges a line shorter than the lower limit into the next line, and reports `text_too_short` without calling for the ones it cannot merge (`references/dub.md`「One request per passage」›「Ones shorter than the minimum are still merged.」); what it counts is also the characters to be read aloud, and cues in square brackets are not counted. Source: requirement, the general way of writing said on 2026-11-12.

   Done when: the words to be read contain no speaker name, round brackets or actions; the stage directions that say manner are all replaced with square-bracket cues; proper names, numbers and abbreviations are all words to be read; and when the model has a minimum number of characters, no passage is shorter than it.

3. **Write the cues.** First do what `models/scene3.md`「Where a cue is written and how far it reaches」›「Set the state first, then write the cue.」 says. The dubbing model recognizes several notations for control; use all that the vendor documentation lists, in the right places (`models/scene3.md`「Cues」): for the manner of one spot write an acting direction in square brackets; for something that governs only a few words (or a whole line of whisper or slowing down), wrap it in a paired tag; for a few words that changed language, use a language tag; a sudden sound is written at the position where it happens. `dub` hands all of them to the model as they are, by position (`references/markup.md`). At the very start of each line write the character's name first (`[甘雨]`, written in the language of this line's words; for cross-language write a name pair, item 7), placed before all cues (`models/scene3.md`「Put the character name first」; in the pack's own language it is the pack's `name`, written the same). Write following `models/scene3.md`「Good examples」: write one when the manner changes, each short and exact; do not write essays, and do not write too few.
   1. Write one at the start, giving the emotion or manner of this moment (when the setup fixes the tone, first see item 6). Each cue is two or three words, always written in English, whatever language the lines are in, and not written as a sentence (`models/scene3.md`「What to write」), for example `[hushed, guilty]`, `[sad and surprised]`, `[holding back tears]`. You should be clear in your own mind why he says this line and what this line is answering, but do not write that into the square brackets: write how it lands on the voice. Whom he is speaking to, what he wants the other to do, what this sentence is doing, and likewise the body's movements, glances, what is in the hands, the scene: do not write them into square brackets; write only what can be heard with the eyes closed (`models/scene3.md`「What to write」). For a whispered remark said close up, wrap this line in `<whisper>…</whisper>` and still write the acting direction before it (`[guilty, nervous]<whisper>…</whisper>`): what is wrapped is one line only, up to the end punctuation of this line. When several lines in a row are all whispered (the whole reply is said under the breath), do not wrap them, and do not wrap them line by line either; write one acting direction at the start (`[hushed, guilty]`): a manner that spans lines or a whole passage is written as acting direction (`models/scene3.md`「Paired tags」). For shouting across a courtyard write `[shouting, loud]`.
   2. When only a few words take a different manner and then it goes back, wrap those words in a paired tag (`models/scene3.md`「Paired tags」): a half-sentence said low `<whisper>…</whisper>`, a half-sentence coaxing someone and softened `<soft>没事了</soft>` ("it's all right now"), one word stressed `<emphasis>…</emphasis>` (`<stress>` also means stressed), two words of a stern order `<forceful>站住</forceful>` ("halt"), a few words said one word at a time `<slow>…</slow>`; they can be written together with the acting direction: `[urgent, loud]我说了<emphasis>不要</emphasis>！<whisper>他们在听。</whisper>`. Write a pair of tags on one line.
   3. Set the strength by the content of this spot: the stronger the cue, the more intensely it is acted (inference: on the first page, one person listened blind; one group heard the difference in strength, and in another group with a different seed the two versions sounded about the same, `models/scene3.md`「Readings from the first-page audition」). For merely displeased write `[displeased]`; write `[furious, shouting]` only for furious. For a line where restraint was kept up front, grief comes up, and it is held down again with effort, follow the item that was deleted from the second example of `models/scene3.md`「Good examples」 (inference, only this one line).
   4. Sudden sounds (a sigh, a laugh, a breath drawn in) are written at the position where they happen, and you may write what kind of sound it is: `[sighs]`, `[small laugh]`, `[shaky breath]` (`models/scene3.md`「Sudden sounds and pauses」, where the leads also are). Do not write onomatopoeic words: do not write `[嘿嘿地笑]` ("laughing heh-heh"); an onomatopoeic word in a cue may be read aloud (transcription, model judgment, nobody has checked; `models/scene3.md`「What to write」).
   5. Turn the acting style of the dubbing guidance into cues in this line: read `### 演法` (acting style), pick out what applies to this moment, and write it at the matching position. For example, if the acting style says "speaks fast when nervous, the end of the sentence goes weak", and in this line she is nervous, write `[nervous, hurried]` at the start and `[nervous, hurried, trailing off]` before the last few words of the sentence (the nervousness and hurry are still going on, so they are carried along, `models/scene3.md`「Where a cue is written and how far it reaches」): `[nervous, hurried]我马上就好，[nervous, hurried, trailing off]真的……` (an example of the notation, not tested); where a habit is written ("sighs when tired"), write `[sighs]` at a place that fits. `dub` does not hand the dubbing guidance to the model (`references/dub.md`「How a line becomes synthesis」step 5), so the acting style reaches the model only through the cues you write. Fine vocal production (larynx position, tightness, breathiness, hoarseness) is written before the words that need this production, one place one cue of two or three words, together with the emotion of this spot (`[raspy, tired]`); do not write it as a sentence, and do not pile it into one square bracket at the start (the item on fine-grained acting in `models/scene3.md`「What to write」, where the readings also are).
   6. Setup: `dub` composes one cue from the setup's emotion, style and energy and writes it at the start of every request; if you wrote a cue at the start of a passage yourself, this passage follows what you wrote and the setup's cue is not added. If the setup fixes these, that is the baseline the user decided: the character name also counts as writing something at the start, so when this moment is the same as the baseline, write the baseline as it is after the name, and start writing from where the manner changes; when this moment is different, write yours after the name. The setup's notes (`instructions`, for example the features of an accent) are for you to read, and `dub` does not send them to the model: write what they say concretely in the script, at the place where it happens (for accent follow item 7). The setup's speech rate (or `--speed <multiplier>`, which overrides the setup's) changes speed after synthesis, and speed is handled by it; where the manner itself turns hurried or slows down, write it in the same acting direction as the emotion of this spot (`[enthusiastic, hurried]`, `[gentle, slowing down]`, `models/scene3.md`「What to write」); where only a few words are said this way, wrap them in `<fast>` or `<slow>` following item 2. Do not write fast or slow as separate cues to adjust speech rate, and do not change punctuation, add pauses or add breathing sounds to adjust rhythm: all of them sounded bad when listened to (`models/scene3.md`「Speed and pauses: conclusions from listening」); where the character really stops, write it following item 9. The dry run in step 6 shows what was added at the start of each request.
   7. Accent: the language of the voice follows the language the user chats in, not the reference (`references/character-voice.md`「Language and accent」). When the reference is in another language, do not ask and do not add another reference: write the lines in the target language, write a name pair at the start of each line `[name in the reference language|name in the line's language]` (`[Harry Potter|哈利·波特][this moment's acting direction]line`), the names on both sides and the language hint are written by `dub`, and add `--allow-cross-language` (step 6). When the user wants an accent, write that accent (`[English accent]`, an example of the notation, not tested), do not write a name pair, and add `--no-language-marks`. Square brackets say what to hear, not what to avoid, so do not write negations such as `no`, `not`, `without` (`models/scene3.md`「What to write」). The `[language: zh]`, `[native English pronunciation]` and a whole line wrapped in one `<english>` that were tried before did not remove the accent of the reference (`models/scene3.md`「Cross-language and accent」, listened to by one person); this reading stays. For the character's own dialect (Mandarin with a Sichuan accent), or an accent more obvious than the one above: follow `models/scene3.md`「Cross-language and accent」 and write the lines in the words of that dialect itself; the features are in the dialect item of `### 台词` of the dubbing guidance, and if they are not recorded, first work them out following the same section. Do not write the name of a dialect such as `[带一点四川口音]` ("with a bit of a Sichuan accent"): the model does not know how exactly to pronounce it (requirement, not measured, `models/scene3.md`「What to write」 the first item). A few words of another language inside a line, using a language tag, is a different matter.
   8. When a line has a few words of another language in it (a Japanese "thank you", ありがとう, in a Chinese line, a French phrase in an English line), wrap those words in a language tag: `[happy, bright voice]那就先这样，<japanese>ありがとう</japanese>！` (`models/scene3.md`「Language tags」: the tag name is the English name of the language, in lower case; wrap only the words that changed language). This is an experimental way of writing: what the vendor asks for is the voice of someone who speaks both languages; if the reference speaks only one language, write it the same way, and the effect depends on the voice. When the whole line is in another language, follow item 7.
   9. Add a few more things when dubbing: after writing the lines following the items above, add a pause (write `，[pause 0.6s]` after a comma) and a sigh (`[sighs]`). Do not change the words and punctuation. How to write them, where to pause, the basis, and the scope in which these have only been listened to on lines spoken by one person (one voice speaking Chinese; other voices and other languages have not been listened to) are all in `models/scene3.md`「Add a few more things when dubbing.」.

   Done when: every clause has had its state decided, and every place where the state changes has a cue; there is a cue at the start (or this moment is the same as the setup's baseline and nothing is written at the start); where only a few words change, a paired tag is used; the words of another language inside a line use a language tag; every item in `### 演法` of the dubbing guidance that applies to this moment has become a cue at the matching position; no cue is longer than 4 words (the target is two or three words), none is written as a sentence, and none contains onomatopoeic words.

4. **Decide the reference.** A pack can have several reference segments, each with one reference tag (for example "平常" (ordinary), "愤怒" (angry), "少年" (youth)), listed by language in the pack's `SKILL.md`. Decide in this order, stopping at the first one that applies:
   1. The user specified it (asked you to switch, or switched it themselves): give `--reference-tag <tag>`.
   2. The chosen setup has a `referenceTag`: do not give one, and `dub` uses it.
   3. Neither: look at the script and the situation, at what the character is like in this scene (for example he is a youth and is angry), pick from the pack's tags the one that says exactly this, and hand it to `dub` with `--reference-tag`. If none fits, do not give one, and `dub` uses the default segment of this line's language.

   The reference governs "what he is like in this scene", and the cue governs "how this line is said": a square-bracket cue does not switch the reference. A reference that has been acted is acted more fully, and the timbre drifts a little (on the first page one person listened blind and both groups were like this; for other voices it is inference, `models/scene3.md`「Reference segments」). When the situation has not changed within a dialogue, keep using the same tag, so that after dozens of lines it does not drift.

   Done when: you can say which tag (or the default segment) is used, and which of the items above it follows.

5. **A line timed to the picture** (only when the duration must match the picture). Give the target duration: `--target-duration-ms <milliseconds>` (in a `.json` script write the `targetDurationMs` of this item), and `dub` changes the speed after synthesis to match it, with an upper and a lower limit on the multiplier (`references/dub.md`「Target duration」). This line is to be dubbed into one piece of audio: write it as one line, with no long pause that does not fit in a request, chapter or change of voice in the middle, or an error is reported before any money is spent. Changing speed too much may sound unnatural (inference, not tested), so first estimate by duration to decide the length of the lines; if the report has `target_duration_missed`, change the lines by how much it lists as the difference (delete characters if too long, add if too short), and dub again.

   Done when: the report has no `target_duration_missed`, or the lines were changed per it and dubbed again.

6. **Dry run, then dub.** `node cli.mjs dub <pack directory> <script file> --out <output directory> --adapter <adapter>`, with the `--setup` (or `--no-setup`), `--speed`, `--reference-tag` and `--target-duration-ms` decided above; to mark for the user the lines whose timbre drifted, add `--min-similarity` (step 7). Expressiveness: when the voice has a fixed value (the setup's, or the global value of the whole voice, visible as `pack.expressiveness` in the plan), by default do not give `--expressiveness`, and give one only with a reason, moving about 10 from the fixed value; when it has no fixed value (neither place has one), by default give `--expressiveness`, choosing the value following `models/scene3.md`「Expressiveness」›「How to choose the value」; when dubbing more than one item at once and different values are wanted, use each item of a `.json` script; the numbers are written in that one place only. First add `--dry-run` (it does not synthesize and costs nothing) to see the plan: in the text handed out, cues and angle-bracket tags are in place, and stage directions and names are gone; which setup and which reference segment are used, and what cue was added at the start of each request. If it is right, remove `--dry-run` and dub. All the ways to write the options and the report are in `references/dub.md`.

   If `--dry-run` reports `dub_cross_language` (the reference segment used is not in the language of this line, and nothing was dubbed): do not ask the user and do not add another reference; write the name pair following step 3 item 7, add `--allow-cross-language` and dub again (`references/character-voice.md`「Language and accent」). If the report says the pack has this language but this tag does not (or the language given is not the language of the lines): if the tag and the language were given by you, switch to a tag that has this language (step 4), or do not give `--language`, and tell the user; if the user named it or the setup wrote it, ask the user together whether to switch. When the reference and the lines are not in the same language, add `--allow-cross-language` the first time.

   Done when: in the plan of `--dry-run` the cues are in place, the names and stage directions are gone, and the setup and reference are the ones you want; for a voice with fixed expressiveness, none was given separately unless there was a reason, and then it moved about 10 from the fixed value; where `dub_cross_language` was reported, the user chose a route and it was changed as they chose; after the real dubbing `dub` exits 0.

7. **Read the report, then hand it back.** Look at `dub.json` in the output directory, and in the note handed back, write which places in this run are worth the user's listening. Do not check separately after dubbing: do not transcribe the audio to compare it with the script, do not check what comes after an ellipsis, and do not switch tags and redub on the basis of similarity. The failures and bad-product signals that `dub` itself reports (`failed`, `speech_maybe_unfinished`, `target_duration_missed`) are not covered by this; handle them following `references/dub.md`「Failure and redo」「Whether it was read in full」: they are things that cannot be handed out; `speech_maybe_unfinished` is the exception, as it only reports which passage may have been cut, does not redub automatically, and you tell the user as it is and the user decides.
   - The `pick` of each line: which segment of which tag was used, and why. For those marked as cross-language cloning (`crossLanguage`: for a whole cross-language line, it exists only when `--allow-cross-language` was added; for a block of another language inside a line (`followsLine`, or the pack has no such language), it is also marked `true` without the option, and this does not stop), the timbre itself may be unstable (so for the several voices listened to; for other voices it is inference, `models/scene3.md`「Cross-language and accent」); say so as it is when handing back, and ask the user to listen to these lines.
   - Lines where an ellipsis was used: say so when handing back; the words after an ellipsis are occasionally lost (model judgment, nobody has checked, `models/scene3.md`「Punctuation」); ask the user to listen to these lines.
   - Timbre: when the runtime environment has `speaker-verification`, `dub` measured it, and `units[i].speakerSimilarity` of each line records the similarity `similarity` to the reference used (`segmentId`); if `--min-similarity <number>` was given when dubbing, the line below it has `drifted` as `true`, and if it was not given there is only the number and no mark (details in `references/dub.md`「Comparing timbre」). The number and `drifted` stay in `dub.json` as they are, and are not a reason for you to redub on your own. When the user asks whether the timbre is stable, tell the number as it is; if they hear that it drifted, follow what they say and redub with a different reference tag: `--only <index> --reference-tag <tag>`, with the output directory as before, and the other lines keep the files and references of the last run (`references/dub.md`「Failure and redo」). When the runtime environment accepts a seed, the line that `--only` points at also gets a different seed (the last one plus 1): redubbing with only `--only`, without switching tags, also does not give the same take as last time; with the same seed and the same request the platform states that it tries to give the same result, and a pair was compared by a program and was the same; after changing the seed only this one pair was compared. The timbre of an acted reference may drift (so on the first page, one person listening blind; others are inference); switch toward what this character is like ordinarily (for example if the "愤怒" (angry) segment drifted, switch to "平常" (ordinary), and leave the anger to the cue). Switch in the same way even if the setup wrote a default reference: the default reference is only a default, and in this case the reason in the report is that the caller specified it (`pick.by` is `reference-tag`). If it still drifts after switching, say that the timbre of this line drifted and which tags were tried, and whether to switch again is the user's decision.
   - Whether it was measured is in the top-level `speakerSimilarity.measured`: when the environment does not have this capability it is `false`, and no line has a similarity. You do not have to say it was not measured every time: only when the user asks whether the timbre is stable, or hears that it drifted, say as it is that it was not measured.

   Done when: the note handed back says where to listen: lines with cross-language cloning, lines where an ellipsis was used (if any); where `dub` reported a failure or a bad-product signal, it was handled per `references/dub.md`; you did not transcribe, check ellipses or redub on the basis of similarity yourself; where it was not measured (`measured` is `false`), you did not say so every time, and where the user asked or heard drift, you said so as it is.

8. **Join into one file.** To hand over one audio: `node cli.mjs mix <output directory> --out <file.wav>`, with pauses and speed changes done as recorded in the report; the rules are in `references/dub.md`「Stitching」.

   Done when: `mix` exits 0 and the file was produced.

## Common pitfalls

- A cue written as one sentence: `[眼睛一亮，凑到跟前，满脸堆笑地讨好]` ("eyes light up, steps up close, fawns with a big smile") writes the action and the expression as one sentence. Write the two or three words that land on the voice: `[smiling, softer voice]` (an example of the notation, not tested; step 3).
- Writing what cannot be heard in a cue: the body's movements, glances, what is in the hands, the scene (`[瞪他一眼]` "glares at him", `[把碗推过去，声放软]` "pushes the bowl over, voice softens"), and what this sentence is doing (`[招呼，利落]` "greeting, brisk", `[嘴上嫌]` "complaining aloud", `[亲热，表功]` "affectionate, claiming credit"): write none of these. Write only what can be heard with the eyes closed: emotion and the state of the voice (`[crying quietly]`) (`models/scene3.md`「What to write」).
- Writing onomatopoeic words in a cue: `[小声，嘿嘿地笑，有点不好意思]` ("low voice, laughing heh-heh, a little embarrassed") was dubbed, and one transcription had an extra "嘿嘿" (model judgment, nobody has checked), which may have been read aloud (`models/scene3.md`「What to write」). For a laugh, write it at the position of the laugh (`[small laugh]`) or write the manner (`[smiling voice]`), see step 3 item 4.
- Wanting to change only a few words but writing square brackets (`[emphasis]`, `[slow]`): a square bracket is acting direction and governs from there onward; to change only a few words, wrap them in paired angle brackets (`<emphasis>…</emphasis>`, `references/markup.md`).
- Writing the acting direction as a `Direction: …` line and a `Line: …` line: inference, worse than writing square-bracket cues directly, only model judgment, nobody has listened (`models/scene3.md`「What to write」).
- Putting other lines from the pack after this line in the same request to give the situation: inference, also worse (same section). Do not put the preceding text and the situation into the request, and do not write them as a long sentence in the cue either: turn them into a cue of two or three words for this spot (step 3).
- A stage direction in full-width Chinese brackets (`（生气）`, "(angry)") left in the words to be read: treat it as something that will be read aloud (the platform declares that round brackets are read aloud, full-width ones were not tested separately, `models/scene3.md`「Text in round brackets is read aloud」). When dubbed through `dub` it is replaced by a cue; when you bypass `dub` and call the model directly, replace it yourself.
- Words to be read left in brackets (`我们（还有她）都去`, "we (and her) are all going"): when dubbed through `dub`, the words in the brackets are not read (`unrecognized_directions` in the `notes` of the `--dry-run` plan lists the places that could not be recognized; a bracket in English that contains a word of the vocabulary, for example `(he smiles at her)`, is not in the list, so check those yourself; one not in English, for example the "笑" in `（笑话比赛）`, is in it). Change it to a form without brackets (`我们，还有她，都去`).
- Trying to act out suppressed anger through "voice held down" in a long cue: on the first page one person listened, this version did not follow it, and it was acted as an outburst (only this version, and one short cue per spot was not tested; `models/scene3.md`「What to write」「Readings from the first-page audition」). Write both the emotion wanted and the volume.
